# Heat Alert Causes Clearance Revocation

1. The General Contractor creates excavation 42. All four approvals are pending.
2. The contractor sends directed review requests to Gas Utility, Electric Utility, Telecom Utility, and Municipality.
3. Each independent authority receives the request and reviews only against its own private records.
4. Gas Utility issues a scoped clearance; no underlying pipeline map leaves the utility.
5. Electric Utility independently issues its scoped clearance.
6. Telecom Utility and Municipality return their scoped decisions.
7. The contractor assembles all four active approvals.
8. The contractor delegates excavation 42 to Subcontractor A.
9. Subcontractor A assigns Crew 7, which now believes all evidence permits work.
10. The Weather Service issues a heat alert for County Region 1.
11. The alert is delivered only to Electric Utility.
12. Electric Utility combines the alert with private grid state and suspends electric clearance 42.
13. Electric Utility sends a scoped suspension notice to the General Contractor.
14. The contractor receives it, while Subcontractor A and Crew 7 retain stale local evidence.
15. Crew 7 attempts to begin excavation during the propagation gap. System policy is violated even though its local check passes.
16. The contractor forwards the suspension to Subcontractor A.
17. Subcontractor A forwards it to Crew 7.
18. Crew 7 receives the suspension and correctly refuses to begin work.
